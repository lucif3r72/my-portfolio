CREDORA

Healthcare Credentialing Platform

An in-house healthcare credentialing platform built at Werq Labs to replace a manual, spreadsheet-driven process with structured intake forms and proactive expiration and approval reminders, designed for the credentialing team, not the providers.

UI/UX Design Design System Frontend Development Healthcare
CLIENT Werq Labs (Internal Product)
ROLE UI/UX Designer & Frontend Developer
PLATFORM Web Application
STATUS In Production (Internal, Data-Sensitive)
YEAR 2024
Overview Research Process Design Results Reflection
01 · OVERVIEW

The Problem

Healthcare credentialing — verifying and tracking a provider's licenses, certifications, and approvals — was running on manual, spreadsheet and document-driven processes. Expirations and approvals were slow to track, and the burden fell on the credentialing team, not the providers themselves.

Because credentialing data is inherently sensitive, the product also had to be built and used strictly internally, which shaped decisions around access and how information was surfaced throughout the app.

My Contribution
UI/UX Design Design System Frontend Development Credentialing Workflow Design
02 · APPROACH

How We Solved It

I designed Credora end to end — every part of the application, from the detailed provider credential intake forms through to the reminder system that keeps the credentialing team ahead of expirations and approvals, came out of this process.

Rather than treating reminders as a bolt-on notification, they were designed as a core part of the workflow: a proactive system that surfaces what's expiring and what needs approval before it becomes a compliance problem.

Key Research Findings
01

Reminders Were an Afterthought

Expirations and approvals were tracked manually, meaning credentialing staff had to remember deadlines themselves rather than being proactively alerted.

02

The Burden Was on Staff, Not Providers

The friction of credentialing sat entirely with the internal team managing forms and follow-ups, not the providers being credentialed.

03

Sensitive Data, Internal Only

Credentialing data required the product to be strictly internal, shaping how information could be structured, accessed, and surfaced.

KEY DESIGN DECISIONS
Intake

Structured, Detailed Forms

Every provider's credentials are captured through structured, detailed intake forms, replacing scattered documents with one consistent, trackable record.

Reminders

Proactive Expiration Alerts

The system surfaces expirations and pending approvals ahead of time, so the credentialing team acts before a deadline becomes a compliance issue.

Workflow

Built for the Credentialing Team

Every screen was designed around the internal staff actually doing the work, not the providers, keeping the day-to-day workflow front and center.

Access

Designed for Sensitive Data

Kept strictly internal, the product's structure and access patterns were shaped around handling credentialing data responsibly.

03 · RESULTS

Outcome

Since launch, Credora has significantly cut down the time spent on the slow, manual reminder-and-expiration cycle, replacing it with a structured, proactive system the credentialing team can rely on.

~50% Time saved in the credentialing & reminder process
Proactive Expiration tracking replacing manual follow-ups
Live In production, used internally at Werq Labs
04 · REFLECTION

What I Learned

Designing Credora reinforced that a product doesn't need an external, polished audience to matter — internal tools carry real operational weight, and getting a reminder system right can save a team hours every week.

Working with sensitive healthcare data taught me to think about information architecture as a responsibility, not just a UX decision — every field and every reminder had to earn its place in the credentialing team's workflow.
